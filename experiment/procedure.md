### Procedure

1. Keep all the resistance values between 1 - 10 k&ohm;.

2. Close the switch S<sub>1</sub> to "aa" and  S<sub>2</sub> to "cc" positions and S<sub>3</sub> to "ff" positions. Observe the load current (<span style="font-family:'Bodoni MT';font-style:italic;font-size:16px">I<sub>L</sub></span>).

3. Remove the load by opening the switch S<sub>3</sub> and read the open circuit voltage (or Millman's equivalent voltage) <span style="font-family:'Bodoni MT';font-style:italic;font-size:16px">V<sub>m</sub></span>. 

4. Next, compute the equivalent resistance (<span style="font-family:'Bodoni MT';font-style:italic;font-size:16px">R<sub>m</sub></span>) of the network as seen from the load terminals,

 &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp;a. Replace the  220 V source by a short by closing S<sub>1</sub> to "bb" and S<sub>2</sub> to "dd".

&nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp;b. Apply 110 V at the output terminals by closing S<sub>3</sub> to "ee". Read the voltmeter (V) and ammeter (I) and get

$$ R_m = \frac{V}{I} $$

5. &nbsp; Now compute the load current. Applying  Millman's theorem.

$$ I_L = \frac{V_m}{(R_m+R_L)} $$
